# DNU rulebook

This is the single editable source of truth for the standing instructions behind SITUATION ROOM / Daily News Update. It is published at `https://buddhuza.github.io/dnu/dnu-rules.md` and versioned in the DNU repository. To change it, open `public/dnu-rules.md` in the GitHub repository, choose **Edit this file** (the pencil), make the change, and commit it to `main`. Do not put passwords, API keys, or private article text in this public file.

Use the latest direct instruction from the editor when it changes a rule. Record lasting changes here under the relevant category and change the application or collection workflow as needed. A rule written here does not prove it is enforced in code or that an outlet was checked. Do not add one-off article decisions or one-off coverage windows as permanent rules. The website does not automatically save chat instructions to this file. Editorial rules and site design rules have separate sections below.

## Editorial policy

### 1. Purpose and scope

- Produce a factual daily monitoring update about security, war, conflict, terrorism, defence, diplomacy, external cooperation and politics. Give OSCE news particular attention.
- Prefer developments involving international relations, negotiations, cross-border security and external cooperation over routine internal party politics. Include a major domestic development when its consequences warrant coverage.
- Exclude routine public-health incidents, including accidental food poisoning, unless the event has a clear security or political consequence. A serious tragedy alone does not make a story a security lead.
- Publish article headlines and summaries in English. If a relevant suggested article is originally in another language, translate its headline and factual summary into English and prefix the displayed headline with exactly “(文A)”. Retain the original link and original headline for verification. Do not add an article when a reliable English translation cannot be extracted. Do not include opinion, analysis, commentary, interviews, features, speculative pieces or questions posed as headlines. Exclude headlines beginning with “Analysis”, “Opinion” or “Interview”, headlines containing a question mark, broad topic pages, source-list labels and irrelevant subjects such as leisure travel. Remove decorative symbols at the start or end of retained headlines.
- Exclude headlines beginning with “Why”, headlines framed as expert or analyst commentary (such as “Expert Says”), and stories about Mehriban Aliyeva.
- Exclude live blogs and rolling update pages; select a specific, verifiable news article about the development instead.
- Exclude conditional or broad analytical pieces about what a change of power “can” achieve when they report no concrete development; exclude retrospective analysis and explainers headed “How”. Exclude routine plastic tableware bans, cultural celebrations, traffic-death statistics, accidental school-bus crashes and child-sex-abuse stories without a clear political or security consequence.
- Report facts without opinions or analysis. Do not fill space with weak, repetitive or merely descriptive stories. Select the most consequential recent developments.
- The coverage window is specified for each collection request; otherwise use the last 24 hours. Report date is the date of compilation. Use Vienna time (CET/CEST as applicable) when determining dates and coverage.
- For automatic collection, verify publication time on the original article page, including a date or time visible there. Exclude an article outside the window even if it appears on a current listing. Manually added articles may be of any age: use the page timestamp, then a date inferred from the article or URL, and finally the import time, clearly tracked as such.

### 2. Sources and collection

- Check every website in the provided source list for each full collection, including sources hidden by a browser filter. Source-list categories are for browsing and never limit where an article can appear in the report.
- Use publisher feeds or other free collection paths first. Use Firecrawl for sources without a usable feed and for article pages needed to verify and extract stories. The preferred credit-saving sequence is listing/feed headlines → shortlist by relevance → scrape promising original article pages. A deep or complete scrape must also investigate sources for which the shortlist returns nothing.
- After each full scan, audit every source. Distinguish a technical failure, no accessible listing, no article page, out-of-window stories, irrelevant stories, duplicates not selected. Investigate zero-result sources individually and try another feed, listing page, site search or permitted route. Never claim “no relevant news” simply because a scan returned zero.
- Cache fetched listing pages and original article content when possible. Reuse cached article pages across collections to avoid spending Firecrawl credits twice for the same content.
- Do not use Google News as a source. Internazionale is included for its free Reuters articles only; label those stories “Reuters”. Keep source failures and collection methods available for audit.
- Exclude articles published directly by national governments, ministries, presidencies and other official government websites, including GOV.UK, Turkmenistan government and president.uz. Exclude direct World Bank publications and Bloomberg articles behind its paywall. Independent reporting about government or organisation actions remains eligible. Do not publish or import stories whose source is osce.org or whose article link points to osce.org or any of its subdomains; independent news coverage about the OSCE remains eligible.
- International agencies may supply any section. For a country, organisation or lead heading with multiple stories, aim for no agency to supply more than roughly half when comparable verified coverage from other agencies exists. Prefer reputable international reporting when several outlets carry the same event; select an equivalent account from another credible agency when it improves the balance. Do not add weak or duplicate stories, or discard a distinct major story, just to meet a quota. Use only international news outlets for Ukraine, including Ukraine lead stories: Reuters, AP, AFP, DW, Politico, Euronews, BBC, dpa and comparable outlets. Do not use Ukrinform, APA, BelTA, Armenpress or other regional outlets there.
- Prefer independent or less government-aligned outlets over government-friendly outlets covering the same event, when both reports are sound: Euronews.al over TemA, N1 over Tanjug, and Civil.ge over 1TV. Source diversity applies to leads and to two-story groups as well as larger groups; use no more than half from one agency when equally sound, distinct alternatives exist.
- Use Russian outlets only under Russia, including when the reported event concerns another country. For Russia, use Russian or international news outlets, excluding Azernews, Ukrinform, Kyiv Independent and unrelated regional or Arabic outlets. For France, Germany and the US, use international reporting or reputable outlets based in that country, not unrelated regional agencies. In particular, exclude Ukrainian or Azerbaijani outlets from France and Germany coverage, and Sarajevo Times and Gulf News from US coverage. Do not place Azerbaijani outlets under Armenia or Armenian outlets under Azerbaijan. There are two IPN outlets: label the Moldovan one “IPN.md” and the Georgian one “IPN.ge”. Label Georgia's 1TV outlet “1TV.ge”. Display Local editions by domain, such as Local.fr and Local.at. Normalise sheet agency names as follows: DTT NET → DTT; Albanian Daily News → ADN; N1 Bosnia and N1 Serbia → N1; GreekReporter.com → Greek Reporter; Mezha.net → Mezha; RFE RL → RFE/RL; 1TV Georgia → 1TV.ge; Asia Plus → Asia-Plus; Crude Oil Prices Today | OilPrice.com → OilPrice.com; IPN Moldova → IPN.md; Qazinform → Kazinform; Politico Europe → Politico.eu; vreme.com → Vreme; AOL.com → AOL; NIN.rs → NIN; euroradio.fm → Euroradio; Pan Armenian → PAN; Novinite.com → Novinite; ABNA English → ABNA24; Committee to Protect Journalists → CPJ; Financial Times → FT; Times of Central Asia → TCA; aapnews.aap.com.au → AAP. Apply these names consistently in the website, Word, email and spreadsheet export. Yahoo is a republisher: credit the original news agency, such as dpa, when the article text or an editor identifies it; otherwise omit the automated item rather than calling Yahoo the agency. The Yahoo republication headed “US military strikes cargo ship accused of violating Iran blockade” is editor-attributed to dpa.

### 3. Selection, ranking and duplicate control

- Select meaningful fresh news, with a maximum of ten stories beneath each country or organisation in the regular sections; lead stories are additional to the Ukraine regular-section limit.
- Lead stories are open to major developments from every section and country. Repeated independent coverage of the same event is a signal of importance and makes the strongest factual account a lead candidate; it does not make every duplicate a separate lead. The weekend automation may promote a consequential hard-news event when at least three distinct agencies cover it. Middle East leads can concern any country in that region, with particular attention to Iran-related developments; group them under the “Middle East” lead heading. An international organisation is not a lead subject unless the OSCE story is exceptional. Do not make a Saudi Arabia story a Middle East lead by default.
- A spreadsheet row's position near the top does not by itself justify Lead stories placement. Check its subject against the security and political scope before promoting it; the editor can promote eligible stories with the star control.
- Order lead headings with countries from South Eastern Europe, Eastern Europe, South Caucasus and Central Asia first, alphabetically; then Miscellaneous countries/topics and organisations, alphabetically. Thus Albania precedes Ukraine, which precedes Middle East. With only Ukraine and Middle East, Ukraine appears first.
- Identify event-level duplicates even when headlines differ or stories come from different agencies. Prefer the clearer, more factual, more reputable or international account during collection. When Add, Import or a later FEEDRUN leaves similar stories in the update, keep them visible and mark colour-coded duplicate groups for editor review; hiding or deleting one clears the warning on a lone remaining story.
- Avoid feature-like, broad, repetitive and low-impact stories when a more specific account of the same development exists. Do not choose a source simply to ensure that every outlet appears in the report; audit and report why a source contributed no selected story.

### 4. Placement and heading order

- Place a story by the location of the event or its main actor, not by the source-list category. If Russia attacks a location in Ukraine, place an international-source story under Ukraine. Russian-source stories remain under Russia; Ukrainian-source stories do not populate Ukraine.
- Put statements by US politicians such as Rubio and Trump under the US even when they discuss Iran or a European security deal. Put French presidential-candidate statements under France. Place bilateral statements by Tokayev under Kazakhstan and a Turkish president's statement delivered in Azerbaijan under Azerbaijan. A Krakow shooting belongs under Poland; because Russian sources may appear only under Russia, omit a Russian-source account of that Polish incident instead of filing it under Russia.
- Display “Civil ge” as “Civil.ge”. The editor-directed French presidential-candidate story about an ICC warrant belongs under France even when the available report is from AA; treat this as a specific placement and source exception, not a general change to the France source rule.
- For a story involving several countries within South Eastern Europe, Eastern Europe, South Caucasus or Central Asia, file it under the reporting outlet’s country if that country is a subject. Otherwise use the principal actor or main event location. Do not infer a country solely from a publisher name appended to a headline.
- South Eastern Europe may contain a section-level OSCE story before the country headings when its subject spans the region, as with an OSCE Secretary General visit. South Eastern Europe: Albania; Bosnia and Herzegovina; Montenegro; North Macedonia; Serbia; Serbia / Kosovo. Put Kosovo coverage under Serbia / Kosovo.
- Eastern Europe: Belarus; Moldova; Moldova / Transdniestria; Ukraine. Put Transdniestria coverage under Moldova / Transdniestria.
- File reporting about conditions for prisoners in Belarus under Belarus, even when a foreign government's reaction leads the headline.
- South Caucasus: Armenia; Azerbaijan; Georgia. Georgia’s breakaway-republic stories belong under Georgia / Abkhazia / South Ossetia.
- Central Asia: Kazakhstan; Kyrgyzstan; Tajikistan; Turkmenistan; Uzbekistan.
- Miscellaneous has these subsections, in order: WESTERN / CENTRAL EUROPE; MIDDLE EAST / NORTH AFRICA; RUSSIA / EURASIA; AFGHANISTAN / PAKISTAN; NORTH EAST ASIA; SOUTH PACIFIC; NORTH AMERICA; INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS.
- WESTERN / CENTRAL EUROPE includes European countries, including Iceland and the UK, except Belarus, Moldova and Ukraine. Display countries under that subsection in alphabetical order. Use “UK” for the United Kingdom heading.
- MIDDLE EAST / NORTH AFRICA includes Iran, Israel and the Palestinian territories, Lebanon, Libya, Syria, Türkiye and other pertinent regional countries. Give particular attention to major Iran-war and Strait of Hormuz developments. In Lead stories, label the topic “Middle East”.
- RUSSIA / EURASIA is for Russia. AFGHANISTAN / PAKISTAN is only for Afghanistan news directly connected with Russia or Central Asian countries. NORTH EAST ASIA includes China, Japan, Mongolia, South Korea and North Korea. SOUTH PACIFIC is primarily for Australia; include New Zealand only when the story is exceptionally important, and require editor review of New Zealand imports. NORTH AMERICA includes Canada and the US; use “US” for the country heading.
- INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS is inside Miscellaneous. It includes OSCE, EU and its institutions, UN and its organisations, NATO, IMF, ECB, G7, G20, ICC, WTO, CIS, CSTO, BRICS and other pertinent bodies. Place European Central Bank stories under the separate ECB heading. Use “UN” as the heading. Order headings OSCE, EU, UN, NATO, IMF, ECB, then other Western organisations, then CIS, CSTO, BRICS and other Eurasian organisations. Use the spelling “Organisation” throughout the website, Word file and email.
- Keep countries alphabetically ordered within ordinary subsections even after manual additions and moves.

### 5. Story text

- Preserve the article’s exact factual headline in content while rendering only all-caps headlines in sentence case. Keep abbreviations, proper names and official acronyms capitalised. Do not append a second agency name accidentally embedded in a headline.
- Remove a leading publisher label “EXCLUSIVE:” from DNU headlines in web, Word and email versions. Keep the unaltered publisher headline for the database export.
- In summary mode, follow the headline with a period, then one short factual sentence that adds a concrete important detail from the article, from the first substantive paragraph, especially its opening sentences, without repeating the headline. Finish with the agency name in parentheses linked to the original article. Avoid long, general summaries.
- When importing a spreadsheet, leave the summary blank if the sheet supplies neither a full article body nor information beyond the headline. Do not invent an added detail or reuse an older cached summary for that row.
- In compressed view (headline-only mode), omit the summary and put the linked agency immediately after the headline. Remove a trailing period from the headline.

### 6. Editorial review before publication

- Confirm the requested coverage window and the article-page date for every selected automatic story. Confirm the original link, agency, English text or a faithful English translation, relevance, placement, source restrictions, headline and one-sentence summary.
- Compare stories by event to remove duplication, review lead order and importance, enforce the country cap, and check source diversity. Keep a per-source audit that explains omissions without equating “not selected” to “unreachable”.

## Website, editing and exports

### Report display and review controls

- Show article dates as, for example, “14 Oct 26”, and use Vienna time (CET/CEST as applicable) for other displayed times.
- Duplicate groups remaining visible for editorial review receive distinct colours and group numbers. Remove the warning when only one checked/visible story in a group remains.
- Hide empty country and section headings when their last story moves away. In Edit mode, permit moving any story to another section, country or organisation and creating a missing heading; retain drag and drop and the move control.
- Both summary (extended) and compressed modes use the same compact line height, paragraph spacing and highlighted heading height in web and Word. Compressed mode shows the headline without a trailing period, followed by the agency.

### Editing, adding and navigation

- In spreadsheet import placement review, show each story's delete action as a small red trash icon in the upper-right corner of its card. Keep its accessible label and its existing immediate removal and skipped-row accounting.
- Keep the four placement controls visible from the start: Section, Subsection or country heading, and Country or organisation as evenly spaced dropdowns in the first row, followed by the New country or organisation text field and Use placement button below. Each field and button is one dropdown-column wide; align the new-country field left and the button right. Disable a control until its prerequisite is selected instead of making it appear later. Additional new-section and new-heading fields may appear when requested. Stack the controls on narrow screens.
- In the Import spreadsheet dialog, do not show a sample list of the first five import-ready stories. Keep the ready count, unresolved placement cards, skipped-row details and technical-tab preview.
- Keep the worksheet chooser but omit the explanatory note about available tabs. If `NewsStatus` is unavailable and the dated worksheet is verified directly, omit that routine technical notice from the dialog; continue to warn when an in-progress edition makes the importer load an older worksheet.
- Omit the explanatory footer about importing to the shared update and clearing all stories. Keep the actual import and clear actions unchanged.

- Escape closes the currently open dialog, including login, Add, Import spreadsheet, Move story, Manage site and spreadsheet export results. An import in progress remains open until it finishes.

- In Edit mode, show a simple trash button for each story in place of the check/uncheck control. Deleting removes the story immediately from the editor view and, on Save, from the published update. Keep inline headline, summary, agency and article-date editing, moving and lead promotion. Accept the displayed date format (such as 14 Oct 26) or ISO date, preserve the original Vienna time of day when the date changes, and reject invalid dates before saving. Editing, saving and switching summary mode must preserve the current reading position. Save remains available after the last story is deleted.
- In Edit mode, use a very faint highlight behind the whole editable story text instead of dotted underlines. Tint it red for FEEDRUN, orange for Add and green for Import, without a legend above the stories. Use a neutral tint when the origin is unknown. Keep the highlights subtle in light and dark modes; they do not appear in the public view, Word, email or spreadsheet exports.
- Add accepts headline, text, agency and hyperlink directly. Manually added stories are editorial overrides: do not apply automatic relevance, headline, government-source, placement-source or duplicate rejection criteria to them. Still require a valid link and explicit placement, and flag possible duplicates for the editor. Use a short sentence from the first paragraph of pasted text as the DNU summary; cache all pasted text as the article body for later database export and publish that cache with shared edits. If the article is not English, translate both headline and full pasted text into English, prefix the translated DNU headline with “(文A)”, preserve the original headline for export, then summarise the translated first paragraph. Mark only genuinely translated stories. Its optional Use URL action suggests a headline, agency and date from the link and reads publisher metadata directly when the publisher permits browser access. It uses no Firecrawl. Keep every suggestion editable and leave missing information blank when the URL or publisher page does not reveal it. Suggest a placement that can be changed, and scroll to the placement controls after an entry is prepared. Do not offer Lead stories as an Add placement; an editor can promote a placed story with the star control. Added stories can be checked, unchecked and moved like other stories.
- Import, immediately after Add in the logged-in editor, accepts DNU database Excel exports and similar `.xlsx` or `.csv` sheets. It previews the rows, converts headings, agency links and dates into DNU stories, and reports skipped rows. For SITCEN workbooks, read full `articleBody` text from the matching dated run in the compressed `NewsWork_A` or `NewsWork_B` sheet when a visible Body column is absent. Use a short, relevant sentence from the first paragraph as the DNU summary, falling back to the sheet summary when the body yields no suitable sentence. Cache each available full body for later database export without fetching the article again. Each import adds new article URLs to the current shared update, preserving existing stories and editorial changes; a matching URL leaves the existing story unchanged. Clear all stories in the Import dialog is the only way to empty the update; show that destructive control in a muted red colour.
- Keep imported summaries to one concise sentence in the web, Word and email versions, including editions imported before this rule was added. Preserve an editor's explicit summary rewrite. Prefer a sentence that adds detail to the headline; remove extra feed-summary sentences and filler without altering the cached full article body.
- Each successful sheet import adds available full bodies to the browser and shared article-body caches, preserving bodies from earlier imports. Database export uses available cached bodies and never needs to scrape an imported story again. Skip rows explicitly marked as paywalled or containing a short subscription gate instead of an article; report them in skipped rows. A missing body without paywall evidence can still appear in DNU but is unavailable for database export.
- Import can load the latest completed edition directly from the configured, viewer-accessible Google Sheet without downloading an Excel file. Use `NewsStatus` when readable, but if Google omits it or returns another table, probe recent date-named Morning News worksheets directly and verify that their rows carry the matching edition date. Never import an unrelated default worksheet just because Google returned it for a requested tab name. List predictable news and `_Audit` tabs and known technical tabs; omit Sources and Rejected from the dropdown. Keep the import dialog free of the separate exact-name worksheet control and close it with Escape when no import is in progress. Tabs without article columns show a read-only preview. Recover available full article bodies from online `NewsWork_B` or `NewsWork_A`. If a new run is in progress, offer the previous readable dated edition with a clear warning. Show a clear access error if Google does not expose the sheet to viewers; keep local Excel and CSV import as a fallback. Import only after the editor reviews the preview and confirms.
- Accept multi-tab SITCEN workbooks up to 40 MB. Default to the newest date-only Morning News worksheet. Show every workbook tab name in the dropdown, across all dates, except Sources, SourceRegistry and Rejected; do not limit the list to one week or use an older-worksheets toggle. A tab with article columns may be imported after editorial review; a technical tab without article columns receives a read-only row preview. Map Category, Headline, Link, ShortSummary and PublishedUTC into DNU fields, while reporting skipped rows and the count of actual full bodies. Articles whose section or country cannot be inferred remain in a placement review list, with dropdowns to choose existing headings or create new sections, subsections, countries and organisations before import; they are not counted as skipped. Each pending placement can be deleted from that import, immediately removing its card and body from the import preview without changing already published stories; show it as deleted in the skipped rows count. The placement picker allows a story directly under a section or a Miscellaneous subsection, without requiring a country or organisation; after choosing a Miscellaneous subsection, show “Directly under subsection” as the default in the Country or organisation dropdown. Existing and new country or organisation headings remain available. An excerpt or headline-only row is not a full body. If the newest compressed working-data run is damaged or has a stale chunk after its complete gzip value, recover the complete value or try the backup working-data sheet so the stories can still be imported.
- The published update may intentionally start with no stories. Keep the full configured section, subsection, country and organisation structure while empty, but show no empty headings. Keep Add and Import available to the logged-in editor with a clear import prompt. When scraping, adding or importing stories, display each story under its assigned section and heading; only headings with visible stories appear. On publication of a new empty edition, clear stale browser stories once. Archived wording can assist import; new sheets add to the shared article-body cache.
- The source list opens the outlet website beside the update for browsing. Add lets an editor enter the article details and its URL as a hyperlink. Do not spend Firecrawl credits merely to show a website in that panel. No Grab or Send article to DNU control is required.
- Search finds text within the update. The top header and source area stay fixed on desktop while update text scrolls. On narrow screens hide the Sources area; keep SITUATION ROOM and the dark-mode switch on one line, allow the page header to scroll away while the compact update header stays sticky. Section shortcuts remain one line and shorten to LEAD, SEE, EE, SC, CA and MISCL on narrow screens.
- The GitHub Pages site has a public read-only view and a logged-in editor view. The public view allows browsing, compressed/full view, Download, Email, spreadsheet export and Search, but shows no Add, Import, Edit or Manage site controls. Place Log in next to the dark-mode switch; after login, show editor controls, Manage site, Log out and shared save status. Supabase Auth verifies the editor account, and database policies allow only the authorised email to publish changes. Never embed the password or a service-role key in the website or repository. Public visitors load the current shared edition and receive later edits on refresh or periodic polling. Dark mode is a local viewer preference.
- Keep the editorial policy available at its direct `/dnu/editorial-policy.md` URL, but do not show an Editorial policy link or button on the main public site.
- Do not show a date or time in the top-right page header. The project and website are named DNU / SITUATION ROOM, rather than scrapeandreport.

### Word, email and spreadsheet outputs

- Download exports only the currently visible/checked stories and respects summary versus compressed mode, including the absence of a trailing headline period in compressed mode. The Word file follows the supplied example’s logo, font, colours and section styling. Its filename and title date use the report/coverage date appropriate to the edition. Do not add a coverage note inside Word.
- At the bottom of the final Word page, put a rule followed by the supplied two-paragraph disclaimer. Display that disclaimer at the bottom of the web update before the coverage note. The email action copies the formatted current update, including the logo, to the clipboard and opens a draft for pasting; the subject is “OSCE SITROOM DAILY NEWS UPDATE, DD Month YYYY”.
- Disclaimer text, paragraph 1: “Disclaimer: This product is based on open media sources only and the material used is not always fully validated. Items selected are abridged from the original or are \"verbatim\". All views, opinions and accounts contained herein are those of the original authors, and do not imply official endorsement or acceptance by the OSCE.”
- Disclaimer text, paragraph 2: “We would like to remind all our recipients that SitRoom News Updates are for internal use only and is not intended for further dissemination or distribution. Thank you for your understanding and co-operation regarding this matter.”
- Spreadsheet export includes only visible articles whose full body can be obtained. Continue past failures, export successful rows and show a result count with failures. Keep fetched full article pages available for export when permitted. Columns: Date, Source, Region, Countries, Local Regions, Organizations/Organisations, Themes, Types, Source URL, Heading, Body. Required fields are Date, Source, Region, Countries, Source URL, Heading and full Body. Preserve the database’s expected column header if it requires “Organizations”, while display text elsewhere uses “Organisations”.
- Spreadsheet Region mapping: MIDDLE EAST / NORTH AFRICA → Middle East; WESTERN / CENTRAL EUROPE → Europe; RUSSIA / EURASIA → Eurasia; NORTH EAST ASIA → North East Asia; SOUTH PACIFIC → South Pacific; NORTH AMERICA → North America; INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS → blank. Use each visible article's current section and country or organisation heading as the primary spreadsheet placement, including after a move or promotion to Lead stories. Add other countries or organisations that are substantively mentioned in the article after that placement. Use “US”, “UK” and “UN” in output labels. For Georgia breakaway-republic stories use Countries = Georgia and Local Regions = Abkhazia and/or South Ossetia.
- For a translated story, preserve the “(文A)” marker in web, Word and email headlines. Spreadsheet Heading retains the original publisher headline; spreadsheet Body uses the English translation when a complete translated body is available. If the body translation is unavailable, count that row as unavailable and continue exporting the remaining stories.
- When publishing the static website, retain previously published hashed JavaScript and CSS assets so open browser tabs can still load the Add dialog and other deferred modules after the site updates.

## Collection commands and publishing

- **NEWSRUN** means a full collection for the last 24 hours ending when the run begins in Europe/Vienna, unless another window is specified. Check all configured sources; use publisher feeds and listings first, then Firecrawl for promising article pages where needed. Investigate zero-result outlets, reuse cached pages, verify article-page dates, audit each source, apply the editorial rules, rebuild the prepared edition, and publish the website and shared Supabase edition when server credentials are available. Report the window, source and story counts, unresolved outlets and any publication blocker. Do not claim a complete run if one did not occur.
- **FEEDRUN** means a last-24-hours collection from outlets with direct publisher feeds in `server/feeds.js`. Use no Firecrawl, Google News or third-party crawler. Shortlist feed headlines, open publisher article pages directly to confirm the publication time and read the opening paragraphs, investigate stale or failed feeds through other direct publisher routes, audit each feed outlet, apply the editorial rules, and append new article URLs to the prepared and shared editions while retaining older stories. Preserve existing editor changes, exclusions and full article bodies when publishing. If Supabase publishing credentials are unavailable, state that the shared public edition still shows its prior content and provide the new edition for editor import.
- Sheet import is the primary manual way to populate DNU. Add, Import and FEEDRUN leave existing stories in place. Only the explicit **Clear all stories** action empties the update; it keeps the section structure and article-body cache. A later FEEDRUN may add its newly selected stories after a clear, but must not resurrect older stories solely from a bundled edition.
- The unattended Saturday and Sunday edition is a separate scheduled workflow. It starts at 07:00 Europe/Vienna and publishes a fresh edition from that day's date-named Morning News worksheet in the configured Google Sheet. If `NewsStatus` says COMPLETE for that date, use it; if status is unavailable or stale, require the current dated tab to remain unchanged across two reads at least one minute apart, after 07:45 Vienna time for a scheduled run. Do not substitute an older worksheet. Email delivery is optional and must not block website publication; when configured, submit the formatted edition through the authorised Microsoft 365 Outlook account to the configured mailing lists at 08:00, never late. This weekend replacement preserves the configured site layout but replaces the previous stories, removes manual stories and old exclusions, and retains full bodies supplied by the sheet for later export. Record how many rows were selected, skipped, unplaced and removed as duplicates.
- In the unattended weekend edition, compare stories by event. If the duplicate check matches a regional account with a major international agency account, keep the international one. Retain different events even when they share a country or topic. Do not send automatically if there are no eligible stories or the email configuration is incomplete. A manual import continues to append stories and require review.
- During weekend automation, resolve every otherwise eligible row needing placement automatically. Check country names, nationalities, capitals and other prominent cities in the headline, with the sheet's region and suggested heading as context; preserve the regional source-home rule for cross-border stories. When no geographic clue is available, place the story directly under the sheet's regional heading or Miscellaneous. Apply normal source-placement restrictions and report any story excluded by those restrictions separately. Manual imports continue to offer placement review.
- Weekend email delivery may use either Microsoft Graph in the GitHub runner or classic Outlook on one configured Windows computer. Windows mode publishes the shared edition in GitHub Actions but leaves sending to the signed-in Outlook profile; never activate both sending routes for the same edition. The Windows sender reads the published visible stories and uses the same formatted email template, subject, logo and disclaimer as the web email function. Previewing must not send or mark an edition sent. Scheduled sending requires today's prepared weekend edition and an 08:00 Vienna time guard.
- Full article bodies are kept in the article cache for later database export when available. Do not claim a headline-only, excerpt-only, paywalled or otherwise unavailable body is a full article. Publicly published body snapshots contain extracted text, never Firecrawl credentials or raw secret-bearing scrape data.
- A collection command also requires publishing the shared Supabase edition for all visitors to see it. A GitHub Pages deployment alone updates application code and bundled files; if Supabase still holds the previous shared edition, visitors continue to see that edition. Editor changes made in the logged-in site save to Supabase and reach public viewers on load or refresh.
- Keep a source-level audit with distinct outcomes for unreachable, unverified, irrelevant, duplicate and otherwise unselected articles. Do not describe an unselected outlet as inaccessible without evidence. Do not claim to have checked an outlet or completed an edition unless the run log supports it.

## Editing this rulebook

1. Open the [rulebook in GitHub](https://github.com/Buddhuza/dnu/blob/main/public/dnu-rules.md) and use the pencil button to edit. GitHub requires repository write access.
2. Put article selection, source and placement rules under **Editorial policy**; put layout, controls and exports under **Website, editing and exports**; put batch workflow rules under **Collection commands and publishing**.
3. Commit the change. The rules file is published with the website deployment. If the matching behavior is implemented in code, update and deploy that code as well; changing this Markdown file alone cannot change the application or the next collection script.
