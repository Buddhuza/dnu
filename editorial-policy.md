# Daily News Update: editorial and product policy

This is the durable brief for SITUATION ROOM / DNU. It consolidates the current instructions from the project conversation as of 23 October 2026. Use it when preparing an update or changing the application, including from a different ChatGPT account. Later instructions supersede earlier ones. This document records the intended rules; the collection scripts and browser may still need changes to enforce a rule. Do not treat a displayed policy as proof that a source was successfully checked.

## 1. Purpose and scope

- Produce a factual daily monitoring update about security, war, conflict, terrorism, defence, diplomacy, external cooperation and politics. Give OSCE news particular attention.
- Prefer developments involving international relations, negotiations, cross-border security and external cooperation over routine internal party politics. Include a major domestic development when its consequences warrant coverage.
- Use English-language articles only. Do not include opinion, analysis, commentary, features, speculative pieces or questions posed as headlines. Exclude headlines beginning with “Analysis” or “Opinion”, headlines containing a question mark, and irrelevant subjects such as leisure travel.
- Report facts without opinions or analysis. Do not fill space with weak, repetitive or merely descriptive stories. Select the most consequential recent developments.
- The coverage window is specified for each collection request; otherwise use the last 24 hours. Report date is the date of compilation. Show article dates as, for example, “14 Oct 26”; use Vienna time (CET/CEST as applicable) for times elsewhere.
- For automatic collection, verify publication time on the original article page, including a date or time visible there. A listing timestamp alone is insufficient for a verified-window story. Exclude an article outside the window even if it appears on a current listing. Manually added articles may be of any age: use the page timestamp, then a date inferred from the article or URL, and finally the import time, clearly tracked as such.

## 2. Sources and collection

- Check every website in the provided source list for each full collection, including sources hidden by a browser filter. Source-list categories are for browsing and never limit where an article can appear in the report.
- Use publisher feeds or other free collection paths first. Use Firecrawl for sources without a usable feed and for article pages needed to verify and extract stories. The preferred credit-saving sequence is listing/feed headlines → shortlist by relevance → scrape promising original article pages. A deep or complete scrape must also investigate sources for which the shortlist returns nothing.
- After each full scan, audit every source. Distinguish a technical failure, no accessible listing, no article page, no verified date, out-of-window stories, irrelevant stories, duplicates, and relevant stories not selected. Investigate zero-result sources individually and try another feed, listing page, site search or permitted route. Never claim “no relevant news” simply because a scan returned zero.
- Cache fetched listing pages and original article content when possible. Reuse cached article pages across collections to avoid spending Firecrawl credits twice for the same content. A new scrape rebuilds the update using all eligible stories from the current and prior cached batches that fall within the requested window; it drops expired stories and clears browser-added stories from the prepared edition. Manual rescraping and adding an older URL remain possible.
- Do not use Google News as a source. Internazionale is included for its free Reuters articles only; label those stories “Reuters”. Keep source failures and collection methods available for audit.
- International agencies may supply any section. Diversify sources across an update where suitable and prefer reputable international reporting when several outlets carry the same event. For Ukraine, especially lead stories, rely predominantly on Reuters, AP, AFP, DW, Politico, Euronews, BBC, dpa and comparable international outlets; do not use Ukrainian outlets under Ukraine.
- Use Russian outlets only under Russia, including when the reported event concerns another country. Do not place Azerbaijani outlets under Armenia or Armenian outlets under Azerbaijan. There are two IPN outlets: label the Georgian one “InterPressNews”. Display Local editions by domain, such as Local.fr and Local.at.

## 3. Selection, ranking and duplicate control

- Select meaningful fresh news, with a maximum of ten stories beneath each country or organisation in the regular sections; lead stories are additional to the Ukraine regular-section limit.
- Lead stories normally have three or four of the most important Ukraine stories and one or two major Middle East stories. Other especially important regional developments may also become leads. An international organisation is not a lead subject unless the OSCE story is exceptional. Do not make a Saudi Arabia story a Middle East lead by default.
- Order lead headings with countries from South Eastern Europe, Eastern Europe, South Caucasus and Central Asia first, alphabetically; then Miscellaneous countries/topics and organisations, alphabetically. Thus Albania precedes Ukraine, which precedes Middle East. With only Ukraine and Middle East, Ukraine appears first.
- Identify event-level duplicates even when headlines differ or stories come from different agencies. Prefer the clearer, more factual, more reputable or international account. Avoid several stories conveying the same fact, such as multiple notices of one diplomatic trip.
- Where possible duplicates remain visible for editorial review, mark each duplicate group with a distinct colour and group number. Remove the warning when only one checked/visible story in that group remains.
- Avoid feature-like, broad, repetitive and low-impact stories when a more specific account of the same development exists. Do not choose a source simply to ensure that every outlet appears in the report; audit and report why a source contributed no selected story.

## 4. Placement and heading order

- Place a story by the location of the event or its main actor, not by the source-list category. If Russia attacks a location in Ukraine, place an international-source story under Ukraine. Russian-source stories remain under Russia; Ukrainian-source stories do not populate Ukraine.
- For a story involving several countries within South Eastern Europe, Eastern Europe, South Caucasus or Central Asia, file it under the reporting outlet’s country if that country is a subject. Otherwise use the principal actor or main event location. Do not infer a country solely from a publisher name appended to a headline.
- South Eastern Europe: Albania; Bosnia and Herzegovina; Montenegro; North Macedonia; Serbia; Serbia / Kosovo. Put Kosovo coverage under Serbia / Kosovo.
- Eastern Europe: Belarus; Moldova; Moldova / Transdniestria; Ukraine. Put Transdniestria coverage under Moldova / Transdniestria.
- South Caucasus: Armenia; Azerbaijan; Georgia. Georgia’s breakaway-republic stories belong under Georgia; in spreadsheet exports put Abkhazia or South Ossetia in Local Regions as appropriate. The older Georgia / Abkhazia / South Ossetia heading should not be used for new output.
- Central Asia: Kazakhstan; Kyrgyzstan; Tajikistan; Turkmenistan; Uzbekistan.
- Miscellaneous has these subsections, in order: WESTERN / CENTRAL EUROPE; MIDDLE EAST / NORTH AFRICA; RUSSIA / EURASIA; AFGHANISTAN / PAKISTAN; NORTH EAST ASIA; NORTH AMERICA; INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS.
- WESTERN / CENTRAL EUROPE includes European countries, including Iceland and the UK, except Belarus, Moldova and Ukraine. Display countries under that subsection in alphabetical order. Use “UK” for the United Kingdom heading.
- MIDDLE EAST / NORTH AFRICA includes Iran, Israel and the Palestinian territories, Lebanon, Libya, Syria, Türkiye and other pertinent regional countries. Give particular attention to major Iran-war and Strait of Hormuz developments. In Lead stories, label the topic “Middle East”.
- RUSSIA / EURASIA is for Russia. AFGHANISTAN / PAKISTAN is only for Afghanistan news directly connected with Russia or Central Asian countries. NORTH EAST ASIA includes China, Japan, Mongolia, South Korea and North Korea. NORTH AMERICA includes Canada and the US; use “US” for the country heading.
- INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS is inside Miscellaneous. It includes OSCE, EU and its institutions, UN and its organisations, NATO, IMF, G7, G20, ICC, WTO, CIS, CSTO, BRICS and other pertinent bodies. Use “UN” as the heading. Order headings OSCE, EU, UN, NATO, IMF, then other Western organisations, then CIS, CSTO, BRICS and other Eurasian organisations. Use the spelling “Organisation” throughout the website, Word file and email.
- Keep countries alphabetically ordered within ordinary subsections even after manual additions and moves. Hide empty country and section headings when their last story moves away. In Edit mode, permit moving any story to another section, country or organisation and creating a missing heading; retain drag and drop and the move control.

## 5. Story text

- Preserve the article’s exact factual headline in content while rendering all-caps or title-case headlines in sentence case. Keep abbreviations, proper names and official acronyms capitalised. Do not append a second agency name accidentally embedded in a headline.
- In summary mode, follow the headline with a period, then one short factual sentence that adds a concrete important detail from the article, usually its first two paragraphs, without repeating the headline. Finish with a period, then the agency name in parentheses linked to the original article. Avoid long, general summaries.
- In headline-only mode, omit the summary and put the linked agency immediately after the headline. Do not append an unnecessary period to a headline-only headline. Both modes have compact, comfortable line spacing; headline-only paragraphs have a little more separation than their line spacing.
- Show each article’s date after its text, in the format “14 Oct 26”, without an “estimated” label. Show the report compilation date beneath DAILY NEWS UPDATE as “18 October 2026”; abbreviate only if space is insufficient.

## 6. Editing, adding and navigation

- The site lets the editor check/uncheck stories, edit headline/summary/agency inline, move stories and mark a story as a lead. Save exits edit mode and shows only checked stories. Re-entering Edit retains the selection. Editing, saving and switching summary mode must preserve the current reading position.
- Add accepts one or more pasted article URLs and a manual entry with headline, summary, agency and hyperlink. Scraped articles receive a suggested placement that can be changed; manually added articles can be placed anywhere. Automatically scroll to newly scraped Add results. Added stories can be checked, unchecked and moved like other stories.
- The source list opens the outlet website beside the update for browsing. Adding an article uses its copied URL in Add. Do not spend Firecrawl credits merely to show a website in that panel. No Grab or Send article to DNU control is required.
- Search finds text within the update. The top header and source area stay fixed on desktop while update text scrolls. On narrow screens hide the Sources area; allow the page header to scroll away while the compact update header stays sticky. Section shortcuts remain one line and shorten to LEAD, SEE, EE, SC, CA and MISCL on narrow screens.
- The public GitHub Pages site has Manage site disabled because it cannot securely verify a password. Browser-local changes are private to that browser and are not a shared policy or a replacement for the versioned project files.
- The top-right clock uses “dd.mm.yyyy HH:MM” in Vienna time and matches the Manage site text colour and size. The project and website are named DNU / SITUATION ROOM, rather than scrapeandreport.

## 7. Word, email and spreadsheet outputs

- Download exports only the currently visible/checked stories and respects summary versus headline-only mode. The Word file follows the supplied example’s logo, font, colours and section styling. Its filename and title date use the report/coverage date appropriate to the edition. Do not add a coverage note inside Word.
- At the bottom of the final Word page, put a rule followed by the supplied two-paragraph disclaimer. Display that disclaimer at the bottom of the web update before the coverage note. The email action copies the formatted current update, including the logo, to the clipboard and opens a draft for pasting; the subject is “OSCE SITROOM DAILY NEWS UPDATE, DD Month YYYY”.
- Disclaimer text, paragraph 1: “Disclaimer: This product is based on open media sources only and the material used is not always fully validated. Items selected are abridged from the original or are \"verbatim\". All views, opinions and accounts contained herein are those of the original authors, and do not imply official endorsement or acceptance by the OSCE.”
- Disclaimer text, paragraph 2: “We would like to remind all our recipients that SitRoom News Updates are for internal use only and is not intended for further dissemination or distribution. Thank you for your understanding and co-operation regarding this matter.”
- Spreadsheet export includes only visible articles whose full body can be obtained. Continue past failures, export successful rows and show a result count with failures. Keep fetched full article pages available for export when permitted. Columns: Date, Source, Region, Countries, Local Regions, Organizations/Organisations, Themes, Types, Source URL, Heading, Body. Required fields are Date, Source, Region, Countries, Source URL, Heading and full Body. Preserve the database’s expected column header if it requires “Organizations”, while display text elsewhere uses “Organisations”.
- Spreadsheet Region mapping: MIDDLE EAST / NORTH AFRICA → Middle East; WESTERN / CENTRAL EUROPE → Europe; RUSSIA / EURASIA → Eurasia; NORTH EAST ASIA → North East Asia; NORTH AMERICA → North America; INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS → blank. Populate Countries from the actual article and update placement; use “US”, “UK” and “UN” in output labels. For Georgia breakaway-republic stories use Countries = Georgia and Local Regions = Abkhazia and/or South Ossetia.

## 8. Visual design and labels

- The page brand is SITUATION ROOM and the update is DAILY NEWS UPDATE; omit the OSCE logo from the web header. Use a slightly beige light background and a working dark-mode switch. Section headings are uppercase and larger than subsection headings; subsection headings are larger than country headings. Keep country heading size at the established smaller size.
- Countries and organisations have pale blue highlighted headings in web and Word. The seven Miscellaneous subsection headings themselves are not highlighted. In Lead stories, Middle East retains its highlight. Keep the specified spacing: more above main sections other than Lead stories, little below section/subsection headings, and a little more above subsection headings.
- Keep the update column readable rather than page-wide. Use compact top bars and controls. The update controls are Add, Edit/Save, headline-only toggle, Download, Email, spreadsheet export and Search; prefer small blue icon buttons with clear hover labels. Section shortcut buttons are centred and evenly distributed.
- Do not show the removed promotional banner, Google News, a second bottom toolbar, or a “Websites without selected articles” block at the end of the report. Source diagnostics belong in the collection audit, where the reason for each missing outlet can be inspected.

## 9. Review before publication

- Confirm the requested coverage window and the article-page date for every selected automatic story. Confirm the original link, agency, English language, relevance, placement, source restrictions, headline and one-sentence summary.
- Compare stories by event to remove duplication, review lead order and importance, enforce the country cap, and check source diversity. Keep a per-source audit that explains omissions without equating “not selected” to “unreachable”.
- Build and review both web and Word versions. Verify the currently selected mode controls Word and email content, and that spreadsheet failures do not prevent successful rows from exporting.
